About AfHA

An Africa-led humanitarian institution

The African Humanitarian Agency (AfHA) is established to strengthen Africa's capacity to anticipate, prevent and respond to humanitarian crises while promoting a coordinated, effective and Africa-led humanitarian response.

Through its mandate, AfHA works to support Member States and humanitarian actors in addressing the needs of populations affected by crises and emergencies across the continent.

Background

Towards stronger African humanitarian action

The establishment of AfHA forms part of efforts to strengthen continental humanitarian capacity and promote a more coordinated and effective response to humanitarian crises in Africa.

A continental humanitarian response

At the 26th Ordinary Session of the Assembly of Heads of State and Government of the African Union, held in Addis Ababa on 30–31 January 2016, the African Union decided to establish the African Humanitarian Agency and adopted the Common African Position on Humanitarian Effectiveness.

Establishment

From continental commitment to institutional framework

AfHA's institutional development is supported by continental decisions and normative frameworks establishing the basis for its role in humanitarian action.

2016

Decision to establish AfHA

During the 26th Ordinary Session of the AU Assembly, 30–31 January 2016 in Addis Ababa, the Assembly endorsed the establishment of an African Humanitarian Agency (AfHA) to streamline humanitarian action and address the changing and complex humanitarian landscape. The Common African Position on Humanitarian Effectiveness was also adopted.

2023

AfHA Statute adopted

On 19 February 2023, the 36th Ordinary Session of the AU Assembly adopted the Statute of the African Humanitarian Agency, paving the way for operationalization and hosting by a Member State.

2024

Host and structure

During the 45th Ordinary Session of the Executive Council, 18–20 July 2024 in Accra, Ghana, the Agency's structure and 2025 administrative budget were approved. Uganda was recommended and subsequently elected as host country.

Operationalization of AfHA

Institutional Purpose

What AfHA is intended to support

AfHA's institutional purpose is reflected through several interconnected areas of humanitarian action.

01

Preparedness

Strengthening the capacity of African institutions and humanitarian actors to anticipate and prepare for humanitarian crises.

02

Response

Supporting coordinated and effective humanitarian response to populations affected by crises and emergencies.

03

Coordination

Strengthening cooperation, coordination and complementarity among relevant humanitarian institutions and actors.

04

Capacity Development

Supporting stronger humanitarian knowledge, skills, systems and institutional capacities.

05

African Ownership

Promoting African leadership, participation and ownership in humanitarian action.

06

Knowledge

Supporting access to information, learning, evidence and institutional knowledge for humanitarian action.

An Africa-led approach

AfHA is intended to contribute to a humanitarian system in which African institutions, Member States, affected populations and humanitarian actors can work together to anticipate crises, strengthen preparedness, coordinate response and promote sustainable humanitarian capacity across the continent.
